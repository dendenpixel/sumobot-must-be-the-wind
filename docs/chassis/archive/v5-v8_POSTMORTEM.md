## 1.2 Why v5 cannot just be reprinted

Measured off the meshes directly (not read off a self-check panel):

- **The wheels do not touch the ground.** Saddle floor Z 13.35 + a Ø25 motor can holds the axle at
  Z 25.85; the wheel radius with tyre is 23.40. **Wheel bottom sits at Z +2.45.** The bot rides on
  its rim and skirt.
- **The wheel wells are open holes straight to the arena floor, inside the seal loop** — 3,599 mm²
  against a design leak budget of 382 mm². **9.4× over.** The fan, the ESC, the 30 A wiring and the
  battery upgrade were producing approximately **zero** downforce.
- **The new battery does not fit.** The pack that arrived is 106.29 mm long against a 75 mm bay —
  **31.29 mm too long.**

That is what justified the rewrite. It is also the only time in this project anyone **looked at the
geometry** rather than reading a panel of numbers, and it produced three defects four rounds of
self-checks had missed. See §17 stage 6.

## 2.4 Design drivers that are *not* requirements

State these as preferences so they can be traded, not defended as if they were rules.

- **Centre of gravity.** Den asked for bottom-heavy. The v6 review measured **CG Z 24.78 → 21.18 mm
  at identical mass** from layout alone; the single biggest move was **getting the PCB off the Z 40
  posts (−1.84 mm)**. *But the same review is explicit that **CG is not the loss mode** — tipping
  needs >120 N; the real loss is being wedged and lifted (R14).* Spend effort on the lip and the rim
  before spending it on the CG.
- **"Small core, big skirt."** Den, 11 Sep: the middle should be as small as possible and the skirt
  as large as possible, for downforce area. Downforce = ΔP × A, so area is linear and free.
- **Ballast is the strategy.** Every gram to 990 g is unconditional normal force that does not
  depend on the fan, the skirt or the suction ruling. See §6.4 — headroom is currently **~23 g**,
  down from 75 g, and **the ballast pockets have been lost from the design** (§16.2 LOST-4).

# 4. What this robot actually is

**No previous version of this document described the object.** A cold reader could not draw it.
Here it is.

## 4.1 One paragraph

The robot is a **250 × 250 mm superellipse ("squircle") disc, ~66 mm tall, in two printed shells.**
The **BASE** is a sealed pressure vessel: a continuous 360° wedge skin rising at 15.14° from a knife
rim at the floor to a flat roof at Z 10.90. Underneath that roof is the **PLENUM** — a closed
cavity, sealed against the arena floor by a silicone skirt in a groove, evacuated by a 40 mm EDF
through a Ø58 duct at (52, 0). The pressure difference pulls the whole robot down: *Downforce =
ΔP × A*. Above the roof is the **BAY**, which carries the battery, the ESC, the logic board and the
motors. The **LID** drops over the base, encloses the bay, and carries the ToF sensors, the display
window, the EDF grille and the anti-climb lip. The two drive wheels sit in **wheel wells** — sealed
boxes that are open to the arena below and capped above, so the wheels can reach the floor without
opening a hole into the plenum.

## 4.2 The parts

| Part | What it is | Printed | Mass (v8/v7A) | Attaches how |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sumo_v8_base` | Wedge + plenum + roof + wells + motor cheeks + duct + battery tub + corner pods | PLA, **roof-face down** | 136.2 g | — |
| `sumo_v8_wellcap` ×2 | Open-bottom box over each wheel-well wall stack, sealing the well from the bay | PLA, open side down | ≤ 8 g ea | 4 × M3 into well wall-top inserts |
| `sumo_v8_lid` | Skirt + leaning tower + flat top + anti-climb lip + all sensor/display apertures | PLA, **flat top down** | 104 g | 6 × M3 button head into base pilots |
| `sumo_v8_hatch` | Flush rear panel filling the battery aperture | PLA, flat face down | ≤ 8 g | 2 × M3 thumb screws |

## 4.3 The sealed-volume topology — the rule that governs everything

> **The KNEE (RIM INSET 34.0, Z 10.90) is the sealed boundary.**
> **Inboard** of the knee, the roof plate separates the **PLENUM** (below, at negative pressure)
> from the **BAY** (above, at ambient).
> **Outboard** of the knee, the wedge underside faces **ATMOSPHERE**.
> No feature outboard of the knee can reach the bay without a sealed penetration through the roof.

This one paragraph explains failure #13 (a corner-pod wire hole was put in the pod ceiling, which
faces atmosphere, not the bay), why corner features are hard, and why an opening in the lid above
Z 10.90 costs **zero** downforce — the bay is already at ambient. It was never written down.

## 4.4 Two leak budgets, not one

They are independent and were repeatedly conflated.

| Budget | Path | Cap | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skirt leak** | arena floor → atmosphere, at the seal line | **382 mm²** design | Sets how much ΔP the fan can hold. v5 measured **3,599 mm²** because of the open wells |
| **S2 bay leak** | arena floor → electronics bay | **≤ 60 mm²** | Keeps grit and suction out of the bay. Permitted: the 4 sensor wire holes (4 × Ø4.21 = 55.7 mm²) |

## 4.5 Assembly and service sequence

Nobody ever walked this, which is why the battery hatch arrived mid-build as a new requirement.

1. Heat-set all M3 inserts into the base (roof pilots ×6, well wall tops ×4, duct ×4, pod ×4) —
   **driver access from above, before anything else goes in.**
2. EDF into the duct collar, 4 × M3.
3. Motors into the cheeks, faceplate screws through the well inner wall, 17.00 c-c.
4. Wheels on, from outside the well.
5. Wellcaps down over each well, 4 × M3 each, onto 3 mm EPDM foam compressed to 1.5.
6. Board A (power/ESC) and Board B (logic/TTGO) onto flush pilots with 6 mm M3 nylon standoffs.
7. Battery into the tub, velcro strap through the two 12 × 3 slots.
8. Silicone skirt strips glued into the three grooves (base inverted).
9. Lid on, 6 × M3 button head. Harness = **one 4-way JST-XH** through the lid gland.
10. **Service:** battery comes out through the rear hatch with the lid on — unplug XT60, slide out,
    plug in the fresh pack. Nothing else is field-serviceable without removing the lid.

# 5. Frame, datums and the geometry that governs everything

## 5.1 The coordinate frame

**This was never stated in any brief and had to be reverse-engineered three separate ways.**

```
Units      millimetres
Origin     the plan centre of the base superellipse
Z 0        THE ARENA FLOOR.  +Z is up.
+X         robot FRONT (the 0° ToF bearing).  +Y is robot LEFT.
Axle       both drive wheels share one axle along the Y axis, at Z 23.40
```

Everything in this document is in that frame **unless a print note says otherwise** — and print
notes do, because both parts print upside-down. "Z 10.90 roof face on the bed" means *the part is
inverted and the surface that is at Z 10.90 in the design frame touches the build plate.*

## 5.2 The RIM and what "RIM INSET" means

The outer curve is a **superellipse (squircle) of exponent 4**:

```
|x/125|^4 + |y/125|^4 = 1        →  250 × 250 mm
r = 125.000 on the axes         r = 148.651 on the 45° diagonals
```

> **"RIM INSET d" = that curve offset `d` mm inward along its LOCAL NORMAL.**
> It is **not** a radial scale and **not** a scaled superellipse. On the diagonals, a normal offset
> and a radial offset give different answers everywhere except at 0° and 45°, where the normal
> happens to be radial.

Generate it, never type it:

```python
import numpy as np
A = 125.0
def inset_curve(d, N=200000):
    t  = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, N, endpoint=False)
    ct, st = np.cos(t), np.sin(t)
    r  = A/(np.abs(ct)**4 + np.abs(st)**4)**0.25
    x, y = r*ct, r*st
    gx, gy = 4*x**3, 4*y**3            # outward normal = grad of the implicit form
    L = np.hypot(gx, gy)
    return x - gx/L*d, y - gy/L*d
```

## 5.3 The verified radius table — every outer curve on this bot

Independently recomputed at 2 M samples. **All fourteen values PASS exactly.**

| Curve | RIM INSET | r on the axes | r on the 45° diagonals | **local radius of curvature at the corner** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RIM** (superellipse a = 125) | 0 | 125.000 | 148.651 | **49.550** |
| seal loop OUTER face | 20.0 | 105.000 | 128.651 | 29.550 |
| seal loop INNER face | 23.0 | 102.000 | 125.651 | 26.550 |
| **KNEE** = base collar OUTER | 34.0 | 91.000 | 114.651 | **15.550** |
| base collar INNER | 36.0 | 89.000 | 112.651 | 13.550 |
| lid skirt OUTER | 36.4 | 88.600 | 112.251 | 13.150 |
| lid skirt INNER | 38.4 | 86.600 | 110.251 | 11.150 |

Why the diagonal column is exact rather than approximate: at θ = 45° the gradient is ∝ (x³, y³) with
x = y, so **the outward normal there is also radial**. Hence `r_diag = 148.651 − d` exactly, and
`r_axis = 125 − d` exactly.

### The fact this table was missing, and it is the important one

```
R_corner(inset d) = 2·125/(3·2^0.75) − d = 49.550 − d
```

**The corner radius of curvature collapses linearly with inset — from 49.55 mm at the rim to
11.15 mm at the lid skirt inner.** Nearly every corner-fit error in the whole project comes from
using one wrong value of this. See §15.

**The offset curve never cusps.** Self-intersection begins at inset = 49.550 mm; the deepest inset
used anywhere is 38.4, leaving 11.15 mm of margin. Not a concern — but worth knowing the limit
exists before someone proposes INSET 50.

## 5.4 NEVER interpolate between the axis and diagonal columns

Almost nothing sits exactly on an axis or exactly at 45°. Linear interpolation between the two
columns is **wrong by up to 4.88 mm** (at INSET 34, worst at polar 18.5°). That single error is
failure #16: Board B's corner at (−78, +88) is at polar 131.55°, r 117.593, where the knee is
**113.502** — so the board overhangs by **4.09 mm**, not the 3.59 the register recorded.

**Use the sampled table in Appendix B, or generate the curve. Never interpolate.**

## 5.5 The wedge profile — the primary structure, previously undocumented

It existed only as one sampled point inside the explanation of failure #2.

```
Rim lip underside      Z 0.50  at RIM INSET 0
Rim lip top edge       Z 1.70  at RIM INSET 0        (skin 1.2)
Ramp                   straight, 15.14° along the local normal, IDENTICAL at every bearing
Gradient               tan(15.14°) = 0.27057
Top surface Z(inset)   = 1.70 + 0.27057 · inset      → Z 10.90 at the KNEE (inset 34)
Underside   Z(inset)   = 0.50 + 0.27057 · inset      → Z  9.70 at the KNEE
ROOF TOP               Z 10.90 = the knee height = the base/lid datum
```

Cross-checks that close: the print note "support annulus **1.2 to 10.4 mm** tall" matches the
underside running Z 0.50 → 9.70 above a bed at the roof plane; and failure #2's "at inset 18 the
wedge skin underside is Z 5.37" matches `0.50 + 0.27057 × 18 = 5.37` (the brief used 5.91 for the
skin underside at inset 20 — consistent).

## 5.6 The lid tower profile — also previously recoverable only by arithmetic

```
SKIRT   a VERTICAL wall, outer RIM INSET 36.4, inner 38.4, from Z 10.90 to Z 20.90
TOWER   above Z 20.90 the outer face leans INWARD:
            outer_inset(Z) = 36.4 + (Z − Z_TOWER_DATUM)·tan(lean)
        *** Z_TOWER_DATUM = 20.90.  NOT 10.90. ***
FLAT TOP  a plane at Z 65.6 (EDF top 62.0 + 2.0 clear + 1.6 skin → grille inner 64.00)
          At lean 10°: meets the tower at RIM INSET 44.282
                       → r 80.718 on the axes = 161.44 mm across; r 104.369 on the diagonals
          At lean  5°: RIM INSET 40.310 → r 84.690 = 169.38 mm across
```

> **`Z_TOWER_DATUM = 20.90` is the single highest-value line in this document.**
> v7 used 10.90, which put the tower at INSET 38.16 where the skirt was at 36.4 — a **1.763 mm
> up-facing ledge with a 90° overhang**, which broke the no-support print strategy (R20/R21) for the
> entire part. That is failure #19, and the corrected datum was never written down in prose
> anywhere. **Write it into the parameter file as a named constant.**

The "158 mm unsupported bridge" in the print notes is exactly this. **The original 158.06 figure was
computed with the WRONG datum (Z 10.90).** Recomputed: **161.4 mm at 10° lean, 169.4 mm at rev A's 5°.**
Either way it is a bridge no printer will span — which is why the lid prints flat-top-down.
*This is a live example of the datum error propagating into a "verified" number: it survived an audit
because the arithmetic was self-consistent against a wrong input.*

# 11. Print reality

- **Base**: printed **upside-down, Z 10.90 roof face on the bed.** The plenum, the battery tub, the
  pod pockets and the well interiors all become upward-facing open cups needing no support. The 8
  radial ribs become free-standing walls that support removal never touches — **this matters: Den
  snapped rib legs peeling PLA support off a previous print (R20).**
- **Support is needed in exactly two places**, and prior drafts said "exactly one":
  1. under the wedge skin, an annulus 1.2–10.4 mm tall reaching the bed — tree support, Z 0.20,
     XY 0.60, 2 interface layers, 10 %;
  2. **the three seal grooves, whose mouths open downward toward the bed.** Their 45° mouth chamfers
     exist precisely so that support releases. **Scribe them clear before gluing the strips.**
- **Lid**: printed **upside-down, flat top on the bed.** The flat top is the first layer; the tower
  flares outward going down the print (self-supporting); the lip's 45° upper face is the growth face
  and its flat underside is the last layer of the lip — **a clean square catch edge.** A flat top
  printed skirt-down would be a **158 mm unsupported bridge**; a dome needs multi-stage support.
  That orientation is the entire reason the top is flat.
- **Bed**: 250 × 250 on a 256 × 256 X1C, **no brim, no skirt.** The base is exactly at the limit.
  **The only sanctioned fallback is rim semi-axis 125 → 120 — and that re-derives every radius in
  §5, drops plenum area ~8 %, and changes the mass and the wheel-well fit. It is a full re-solve.
  Decide before any layout work, not after.**
- **Do not split the base.** It is the pressure vessel.
- **Coupons before anything real**: hole shrinkage (4.00 / 4.15 / 4.30), cheek bore
  (25.1 / 25.3 / 25.5), groove (1.25 / 1.30 / 1.35). **Shrinkage coupon FIRST** — it sets
  `hole_shrink` for every other number on the bot.
- Print profile as used for the 0.82 mass factor: **PLA, 0.20 layers, 4 walls, 15 % gyroid.**

# 13. Why it kept happening — the taxonomy

Prior drafts offered five classes. Mapped against the register, **those five explain 17 of 46
failures (37 %)**, and two of the five have exactly **one member each** — they are anecdotes promoted
to categories. Worse: all five describe mistakes *inside the artefact*, and **none describes a
mistake in how the work was organised**, which is precisely why the same author made them four
times in a row.

Three families. The old five all live in family A.

## C. Process errors — why it happened four times

**This family had one member in the old taxonomy. This is where the repetition actually lives.**

**C1 — Change without dependency re-check.** Lean 10° → 5° fixed the flat-top reach and *instantly*
broke the 6.0 anti-climb lip (#31). Without a dependency graph you cannot know the blast radius of a
change, so the only *safe* move is to re-derive everything — which is a rewrite, which reintroduces
hundreds of hand transcriptions. **The absence of a graph is what made full rewrites feel rational.
They weren't.**

**C2 — No single source of truth.** Five live instances: the **6-vs-4 lid pilot mismatch** (I4 vs
rev A, still unresolved); #38 (two specs, one joint); #24 (skin written 1.2 and 1.6); #3 (bore vs
bore + wall); #19 (skirt Z 20.90 vs tower datum Z 10.90). Across documents too: the project's
`SumoBot_Mass_Budget.md` files the weighed 151.2 g pack under a "3S 1500 mAh" line while the label,
barcode and 24.42 Wh all say **2200 mAh** — the supplier substituted a bigger pack.

**C3 — Cross-part contract drift.** Base and lid were designed by two independent sessions with no
shared interface document. **The two surviving parts are v8 BASE and v7 LID rev A — different
generations.** #19 and #22 are both base↔lid joint failures. §7 exists to end this.

**C4 — Open-loop verification. This is the master class.** The author wrote a brief about a solid he
had never looked at, and every piece of feedback was a text panel of numbers he had himself
specified — so the loop could only confirm *the build matches the brief*. It could never say *the
brief is wrong*. The decisive evidence is in this document and went unremarked for four
generations: **the one time anyone parsed and section-cut a mesh (v5, 11 Sep), three defects fell
out that four rounds of self-check panels had never seen — including "the wheels do not reach the
ground."** That inspection was never repeated on v6, v7 or v8. #13 is the pure case: one section cut
would have shown the pod ceiling facing atmosphere.

**C5 — Scope churn.** Churn does not produce a numbered row; it produces a whole rejected
generation, so it is invisible in §12 by construction. Mid-stream: add 4 corner IR sensors, drop the
lidar, make the top flat, make it bottom-heavy, add a battery-swap slit, mount the PCB vertically,
lengthen the lip, don't snap the ribs. And silently: **the ToF count went from 3 to 6** with no
logged decision — which is what forces the PCF8574, which forces the harness gland, which is part of
what fills the roof that §16 says is too small.

**C6 — Feature-cost blindness. The strongest single finding in this post-mortem.** The corner pods
cost **eight failures** (#11, #12, #13, #14, #35, #36, #37, #44) across two generations, and they are
the sole reason the 11.00 mm diagonal band is a binding constraint at all. **A working alternative
was already documented in the project before the pods existed** — a bare TCRT5000 in a blind pocket
under the wedge skin, open only downward. *(That note quoted v5's two-slope brim at r 108; on the v8
wedge the correct radius is **RIM INSET 24.03 = r 100.98 on the axes / r 124.63 on the diagonals**,
which is where the 7.0 mm the sensor needs first exists. See §16.1.)* Nobody was ever required to
ask "what has this feature cost, and what does it buy?" — so the answer never surfaced, even though
the previous draft of this very document already said *"pods cost the most conflicts of any
feature"* and then recommended nothing.

**C7 — Requirement / specification conflation.** No brief ever separated *what it must do* from *a
number chosen to achieve that*. So a spec could be defended as if it were a requirement, and a
requirement could be deleted as if it were a number. #29 is the pure case: "sensor axis horizontal"
is a requirement; "seat the board flat in the wall" is a spec meant to achieve it; the spec was kept
and silently broke the requirement. The same mechanism deleted the **claw weapons** in eleven words
and the **knock-outs** in one ruling.

**C8 — The author was both writer and checker.** Self-review finds typos, not premises. Every
impossible constraint (#35: ≤ 20° on a 15.14° ramp) is a premise error, and premise errors are
invisible from inside. The only genuinely independent checker in the loop was the generator — which
is why the generator kept finding things.

## 13.1 The one-line diagnosis

**The design had no representation other than English prose, and prose cannot be checked.** ~100+
interdependent numbers were maintained in natural language, which has no type system, no units, no
referential integrity, and no way to ask *what depends on this*. Every consistency property had to
be held in one head, simultaneously, across a document longer than any head holds. **#12 — three
numbers that agree at exactly one radius — is not a mistake a careless person makes. It is the
mistake the medium makes inevitable.**

## 16.4 ⚠ THE BATTERY HATCH DOES NOT WORK — the one live, unresolved blocker

**R16 ("battery swappable without removing the lid") cannot be satisfied by the current spec, and
this was not noticed until the final review of this document.** The arithmetic:

| | |
|---|---|
| Battery sits (I9) | **Z 3.90 … 25.36** |
| Battery tub walls stop at (I9) | Z 13.0 — *so the pack can lift out of the tub after 9.1 mm* |
| Hatch sill, after #22's ruling | **Z 22.0** (it must clear the lid skirt, which runs Z 10.90 → 20.90) |
| Lift needed to get the pack's underside above the sill | **18.1 mm** |
| Headroom above the pack (I12: lid inner ≥ 28.0 over the tub) | **2.64 mm** |

**The pack cannot be raised far enough to pass over the sill.** The original sill at Z 11.0 worked
geometrically and cut 9.9 mm out of the lid skirt — that is failure #22, and "sill Z 22.0" fixed the
skirt by breaking the requirement. **Neither position works. This needs a ruling before either part
is generated.** Three options:

1. **Frame the aperture through the skirt.** An opening above Z 10.90 costs **zero downforce**
   (§4.3 — the bay is already at ambient), so the skirt here is structure, not seal. Keep the sill at
   Z 11.0 and run a 3 mm boxed frame around the aperture to restore the hoop stiffness the skirt
   provided. *Cheapest, and it is the only option that keeps the pack where R17 wants it.*
2. **Raise the battery tub** so the pack's underside sits at Z 22.0. Costs **18 mm of CG height** on
   the heaviest single item — directly against R17, and it eats the bay.
3. **Drop R16.** Swap the battery by removing the lid (6 × M3). Between bouts there is time; mid-bout
   there is not. Ask first — Den asked for this feature explicitly and it is the only requirement here
   that came from him rather than from the competition brief.

**Recommendation: option 1.** It was rejected in v7 only because "cuts the skirt" was read as a seal
failure when it is not one.

# 17. The method for the next session

## 17.0 Stage zero — ask whether to do this at all

Before any CAD: the fan kill gate is **next week** and the first drive is **ten days away and
needs only a rolling platform.** A rolling platform is the base's drive geometry (cheeks, wells,
faceplates, wheels) and nothing else — no plenum seal, no lid, no ToF, no pods, no fan.

**Consider printing a drive-only base first and banking the first drive.** Everything in §7 survives that
decision; it just gets built in two passes instead of one.

## 17.1 Stage 1 — `REQ.md`
Numbered requirements only, each with: `ID | requirement | source | verification method | owner`.
Rule-mandated first and marked non-negotiable. **§2 of this document is the draft, to be confirmed before the build.**
**Done when** every requirement has a verification method and every feature you intend to build names
a REQ id. *A feature with no REQ id is deleted, not discussed.*

## 17.2 Stage 2 — `params.py`, the single source of truth
Three sections exactly as §6: `MEASURED` (with provenance and date) · `ASSUMED` (each with the test
that retires it) · `DERIVED` (**expressions only, zero literals**). Plus `check_params()` asserting
`MIN_WALL 1.2`, `MIN_GAP 0.8`, `MIN_LAND 1.5`, `MAX_OVERHANG 55°`, bed and mass ceilings.
**Done when** `python params.py` prints the full derived table and exits 0.
*This makes A3, A7, A8 and part of A4 structurally impossible — **sixteen** register entries.*

## 17.3 Stage 3 — `envelopes.py`, the layout solver. **This is a gate, not a step.**
Every object is `Envelope(id, solid, z_range, fusions=[], req=[])`, with two hard rules:

- **The solid is the INSTALLED volume**, not the part: part + wall + heatshrink + fastener + driver
  access cone + service swing. *#3, #7 and #23 all die here.*
- **Negative space is declared as envelopes too**: wire corridors, the 8 rib windows, plenum flow,
  battery-swap swing, insert tool access. *#8, #13, #17 and #46 all die here.*

Then extend v8's raster **beyond overlap**. At 0.5 mm compute: pairwise overlap (zero outside
declared fusions) · **min gap** every non-fused pair ≥ 0.8 · **min wall** every cavity-to-surface
≥ 1.2 · **min land** around every hole and counterbore ≥ 1.5 · **absolute slope and overhang** at
every blended face · **leak area per feature** against the S2 cap · **mass and CG** from the voxels.
**Done when** everything is green, every fusion is named *in advance*, and leak/mass/CG are
allocated per feature rather than only totalled.

## 17.4 Stage 4 — freeze `INTERFACE.md`
**§7 of this document is it.** Version it. Both briefs `include` it verbatim. Neither session may
change a number in it; a change is a contract revision and both parts rebuild. *C3 dies here, along
with #19, #22 and D8.*

## 17.5 Stage 5 — the brief is **rendered, not written**
A template that pulls every number from `params.py` and `envelopes.py`. Prose carries intent and REQ
ids only. Each constraint block reads: `REQ-id | the number | where the number came from | the check
that verifies it`. **Done when** `grep` finds no numeric literal in the brief that isn't printed from
the model. *A1, A2, A3 and A4 cannot recur.*

## 17.6 Stage 6 — patch, never rewrite
**The most transferable result of the entire day, and it was one line in the old draft:** v7 lid
rev A used a **modification block into the same chat** — 14 targeted changes — and took the lid from
**23 constraint failures to 10** (31 total findings to 11), **at a fraction of the cost of a rewrite.** Every full rewrite made
things worse or sideways. Default to a patch. A fresh build only when the architecture changes.
Before any parameter change, **print its dependents from the graph** and state which checks re-run.

## 17.7 Stage 7 — close the visual loop. **The stage that never existed.**
Export the mesh. Section-cut at X = 0, Y = 0, the 45° diagonal, and Z at the roof. Render six views.
**Look at them.** This is not optional — it is the only step in the entire project history with a
proven hit rate: **one sitting, three defects, including "the robot does not touch the ground."**
**Done when** Den has seen 6 renders and 4 sections and said yes — *not* "read the panel and said yes."

## 17.8 The human loop, kept cheap
Den gets three question types, each ≤ 5 minutes, and **never a 100-number brief** — he cannot check
one and neither can the author:

1. **After stage 1:** one screen. *"Here are the requirements and the three features about to consume
   budget. Cut anything?"*
2. **During stage 3, only on a genuine conflict:** *"X and Y cannot both exist. A costs this, B costs
   that. Pick."* A choice, not a puzzle.
3. **After stage 7:** the pictures. *"Is this the robot you want?"* A look, not a read.

## 17.9 What to hand the next CAD session

1. **§7** (interface contract) — verbatim, both prompts.
2. **§9** (the verified v8 layout table) — as the starting layout, not a reference.
3. **§8** (the check register) — as the self-check spec.
4. **§6** (parameters) — as `params.py`.
5. **§15** — so it does not inherit the wrong corner radius.
6. **§16** — so it knows what was deleted and why, and what is still undecided.
